## Problem

Crop pathogens do not respect borders. They travel with traded goods and establish where the climate allows. A model that treats each country as an independent time series ignores the main mechanism of spread.

PathoGraph-DL treats forecasting as a problem on a graph that changes over time: countries are nodes, trade connects them, and risk in one place depends on what is happening in the places it trades with.

## Graph and data

Three kinds of signal come together: international trade, climate, and reported outbreak information for roughly eight crop pathogens across about 194 countries.

Getting heterogeneous public data into one consistent tensor is most of the work. Inputs are audited, stored as Zarr tensors, and guarded by strict data contracts so that a silent change in shape, units or coverage fails loudly instead of quietly changing a result.

## Model

The model is a spatio-temporal multi-modal graph neural network (ST-MM-GNN), written in PyTorch and PyTorch Lightning. Experiments are driven by configuration files and trained on Vertex AI, with the code packaged so the same build runs locally and in the cloud.

## Evaluation

The easiest way to get an impressive score on data like this is to leak the future: split rows at random and the model sees the same outbreak from both sides.

Outbreaks are rare, and on rare events AUROC alone can flatter a model. The frozen record therefore reports precision-recall and calibration alongside it:

| Held-out test metric | Mean ± s.d. | Range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Macro AUROC | 0.9604 ± 0.0099 | 0.9528 – 0.9717 |
| Macro AUPRC | 0.7849 ± 0.0579 | 0.7128 – 0.8563 |
| Brier score | 0.0214 ± 0.0033 | 0.0179 – 0.0244 |
| Expected calibration error | 0.0197 ± 0.0036 | 0.0157 – 0.0244 |

The numbers are less interesting than what they were measured against. The forecast is scored on a period the model never saw, with probabilities that mean what they say, from an artifact that can be rebuilt.
