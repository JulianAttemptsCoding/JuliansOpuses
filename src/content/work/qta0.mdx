## Hypothesis

QTA0 is a daily-frequency, cross-sectional US-equity forecaster built as a mixture of experts, written end to end and trained on [Vertex AI](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai). The hypothesis was the usual one: that a learned model could rank stocks well enough to beat holding the index.

## The result that looked good

One early configuration did beat the index. Out of sample from 2023 into 2026, it returned +92.1% against +84.0% for SPY.

Looked at more closely, the result is weaker than the headline. The strategy finished ahead on return with a worse Sharpe ratio and a deeper drawdown, and most of the return was market beta. Underneath was a small, real stock-selection signal: a test hit rate of 52.5%.

## Making the test harder

A single chronological split next to the training period is one path through history. It cannot distinguish a model that has learned something durable from one that fits a regime. So the strategy was re-tested under progressively stronger designs: combinatorial purged cross-validation over many train-test paths, a larger universe, and finally a chronological test placed far from the training period.

| run0 | Alpaca SIP | 83 | Chronological, adjacent | 52.5% (t = +3.49) | Beat SPY on return only |

| run2 | yfinance | 153 | CPCV (10,2), 45 folds | 50.5% (t = +5.91) | Negative |
| run2.1 | yfinance | 153 | Chronological, distant | 49.7% (t = −1.56) | Negative, coin flip |

The hit rates in run1 and run2 are statistically distinguishable from 50%, and still too small to survive trading costs.

## What the decline means

Directional accuracy falls the further the test set sits from training in time. That is the fingerprint of concept drift: whatever the model learned was true of the market it trained on and fades as the market moves on.

The useful output of this project is the conclusion, and the method that produced it: an apparent edge, tested until it was gone, and reported that way.
